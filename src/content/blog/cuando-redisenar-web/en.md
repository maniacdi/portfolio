When did you last visit your own website on your phone, as if you were a customer? Try to book an appointment, find your opening hours or check a price. Do it before reading on. Plenty of business owners find out this way that their site has been working against them for years. And a website redesign, when it's due, is mostly about no longer losing customers who never get as far as calling.

These are the seven signs I'd look for. They don't all need to apply; two or three are already reason enough to think about it.

## 1. It's slow to load

If your site takes several seconds to show anything useful on a phone, some people leave before they see it. And they don't come back. Google measures it too, and takes it into account when ranking you. I explain why, and how to measure it, in [a fast website ranks better](/en/blog/web-rapida-mejor-seo).

You can check for free with Google's PageSpeed Insights: enter your address and it tells you how the site performs on mobile. If it's all red, that's your first sign.

## 2. It looks wrong or is awkward on a phone

Text you have to pinch to read, buttons so close together you hit the wrong one, menus that won't open, a phone number you can't tap to call. For a local business, a large share of visits come from phones. If the site was designed for desktop and "adapted" afterwards, it shows.

## 3. Nobody updates it because it's a hassle

Opening hours from two years ago, old prices, a news section whose last post is from 2021. It's rarely laziness: changing anything is so awkward that it keeps getting put off. This is the clearest sign that the problem lies in how the site is built, more than in how it looks, and it's the one that most justifies a proper rebuild.

## 4. It doesn't show up on Google when people search for what you do

Search for your service and your town ("physio in Santander", "builders in Leeds") in a private window. If you're nowhere to be seen, and your site is a single page with every service lumped together, or has page titles like "Home" and "Welcome", the structure is holding you back. Sometimes that can be fixed without a redesign, but if the site doesn't let you create a page per service, it's time to rebuild.

## 5. An outdated website that no longer looks like your business

You've moved premises, added new services, the team has changed or the brand has evolved. And the website still describes the business you were five years ago. An outdated website looks old and, on top of that, gives wrong information, which breeds distrust at the worst moment: when someone is deciding whether to choose you.

## 6. You get very few enquiries through it

If you get visits but hardly anyone calls, writes or books through the site, something's going wrong along the way. Usually the main action isn't visible, the form goes on forever or the site doesn't answer what people are looking for. Check your Google Search Console and analytics data: are people arriving and leaving without doing anything?

## 7. It depends on something you no longer control

The developer who built it no longer replies, nobody knows the admin password, the theme no longer gets updates or the domain is in someone else's name. You can't see this from the outside, but it's the most dangerous sign of all: the day something breaks, there'll be nobody to fix it.

## Website redesign or a refresh: how to decide

Not everything is fixed by a new website, and you don't always need one. My rule of thumb:

- If the problems are mainly content (outdated copy, poor photos, missing information), refreshing the content on the same site is often enough.
- If the problems are speed, mobile, structure or how it's edited (signs 1, 2, 3 and 7), tweaking usually means patching. A proper redesign tends to pay off better than paying for fix after fix.

Whatever you do, don't lose what already works. If your current site has pages that rank on Google, a careless redesign can wipe out that visibility overnight. Changing URLs without redirecting them is the classic mistake. I explain how to do it properly in [migrating a website without losing SEO](/en/blog/migrar-web-sin-perder-seo).

## Where I'd start

Take this list, open your website on your phone and count how many signs apply. If it's one or two, try fixing them yourself or with a small job. If it's three or more, a redesign is worth discussing.
