I recently reviewed the SEO on my own website and found something I'd call out on any client's site: the homepage title didn't mention Santander anywhere. I work in Santander, I look for clients in Santander… and I was hiding it from Google. So I fixed it, and while I was at it I put together everything I know about local SEO for small businesses.

Local SEO is what decides whether you show up when someone searches for "physio in Torrelavega" or "restaurant near me". It isn't the SEO of a newspaper or a shop selling nationwide: here you compete with the businesses in your area, and the rules change.

## How Google decides who to show

Google says it openly: for local results it looks at three things.

- Relevance: whether what you offer matches what's being searched.
- Distance: how close you are to the person searching (or to the area they name).
- Prominence: how well known your business is. Reviews, mentions on other websites, your own website.

You can't change the distance. You can work on the other two, and almost everything below feeds one of them.

## Your Google profile matters more than your website

When you search for a business in your city, the first thing you usually see is a map with three listings. That's Google Business Profile, and for a local business it's probably the best return you'll get on an afternoon's work.

The minimum: a well-chosen primary category (as specific as possible, not "Company"), real opening hours kept up to date on holidays, phone number, real photos and a link to your website. A half-finished profile tells Google the business is half-finished.

If you don't serve customers at your premises (a plumber, a photographer, me), you can hide the address and set the area you cover instead. Google allows it and doesn't penalise it.

## Reviews: volume, consistency and replies

Reviews count towards prominence and, above all, towards someone choosing you over the place next door. What works is no mystery: ask for them. At the right moment, when the customer leaves happy, with a direct link or a QR code at the counter.

And reply to all of them, including the bad ones. A polite reply to a negative review says more about your business than ten five-star ones. What you should never do is buy them: Google detects them, removes them and can suspend your profile. Not worth it.

## Your website has to say where you are

This is where many business websites fall short, mine included until recently. Google doesn't guess where you work; it reads it. And it reads it in specific places:

- The title of each page: "Dental clinic in Santander", not "Home".
- One page per service, each with its city where it makes sense. A single page listing every service competes for everything and wins nothing.
- Name, address and phone number written the same way on your website, your Google profile and any directory. "3 Burgos St" in one place and "Calle de Burgos 3, ground floor" in another is the same thing to you. To a machine, not so much.
- Fast loading on mobile, because most local searches happen on a phone and in a hurry. I explain why in [a fast website ranks better](/en/blog/web-rapida-mejor-seo).

If you're building your website from scratch, [how to create a website for your small business](/en/blog/como-crear-pagina-web-negocio) covers the order I'd do it in.

## Structured data: speaking Google's language

Structured data is a set of tags visitors don't see, but Google does. They tell it, without ambiguity: this is a business, it's called this, it's in this city, it opens at these times and offers these services. The standard format is Schema.org, and there are specific types for local businesses (LocalBusiness and its variants: Dentist, Restaurant, ProfessionalService…).

It won't work miracles on its own, but it removes doubt. I use it on every service page of this website, and it's one of the first things I check whenever I work on a site. WordPress can do it with plugins; with a [custom website](/en/blog/web-a-medida-vs-wordpress) you control exactly what is said and where.

## Mentions outside your website

Other websites talking about you counts too: the directory of your local business association, the local paper that ran a story, a supplier's website listing you as a customer. No need to obsess over "getting links". You need to be where a customer in your area would expect to find you, with your details written correctly.

## What doesn't work (even if someone sells it to you)

- Cloned pages for every town in the area with only the name changed. Google recognises them as filler.
- Repeating "dentist Santander" ten times in a paragraph. It reads terribly and adds nothing.
- Fake or bought reviews, for the reasons above.
- Paying someone who cold-calls you to "put you first on Google Maps". Google doesn't sell those spots.

## How long until you see results

It depends on the competition in your area and where you're starting from. Changes to your Google profile usually show sooner than changes to your website, which can take weeks or a couple of months. What matters is measuring: Google Search Console tells you which searches you appear for, and your profile itself shows how many people asked for directions or called you from it.

My advice, if you only have one afternoon: complete your Google profile 100% and check that your homepage title says what you do and where. It's what I took too long to do on my own site.
