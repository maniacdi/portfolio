You run a business, you see your customers glued to their phones and you think: "I need an app". Before you ask for a quote, try a quick test. Take out your own phone and count how many apps from local businesses you have installed. Three? None? That's the real competition for a spot on your customer's screen. The app vs website for business question comes down to a few questions, and hardly any of them are technical.

Here they are, in the order I'd ask them.

## How often will customers use you?

This is the one that matters most. An app has to be found in the store, downloaded, given permissions and sometimes signed up for. That effort only pays off if it then gets used a lot.

A hairdresser you visit every six weeks, a restaurant, a clinic, a garage: customers search for you on Google when they need you, check opening hours, call or book. For that, a fast, well-built mobile website beats an app, because you're not asking them for anything in return.

A gym where you book classes three times a week, an ordering service you use daily, a work tool for your team: that's where an app starts to make sense, because tapping an icon is quicker than looking up a website every time.

## Do people who don't know you yet need to find you?

If what you want is new customers, an app won't help. Google doesn't index the inside of an app the way it indexes a website, and nobody downloads the app of a business they've never heard of. New customers come from your website, your Google Business Profile and word of mouth.

So in most cases the logical order is website first and then, if needed, the app. Almost never the other way round.

## Do you need to work offline?

If your team works in basements, warehouses, building sites or roads with no signal, and needs to log data there, an app handles it better: it saves on the phone and syncs when the signal comes back. My gym app works that way because the gym Wi-Fi always fails.

A website can do something similar with modern techniques, but it's more limited and less reliable, especially on iPhone.

## Do you need the phone's features?

Push notifications, Bluetooth to connect to a device, NFC, background location, deep access to the camera or contacts. A website can use some of these (the camera or location while it's open, for instance), but full, reliable access to the phone's features is still app territory.

If your idea depends on any of them, lean towards an app. If you only need to show information, forms and bookings, a website does it without breaking a sweat.

## App vs website for business: the practical differences

Beyond what each can do, some practical things change:

| | Website | App |
|---|---|---|
| How customers get there | Google, a link, a QR code | Download from the store |
| Updates | Instant for everyone | Go through store review (except minor changes in some cases) |
| Upfront cost | Lower | Higher |
| Maintenance | Server and updates | The same, plus new iOS and Android versions |
| Fees | No store fees | Stores take a cut of certain in-app payments |

In the mobile app or website debate, neither column wins across the board. That's why it's a business decision, and not a fashion one.

## There's a middle ground

Between a regular website and a store app there's an option worth knowing about: the PWA, a website that installs on the phone with its own icon and can partly work offline. It isn't right for everything and it has clear limits on iPhone, but for some businesses it's exactly what they need. I compare them in depth in [PWA or native app](/en/blog/pwa-vs-app-nativa).

## Do I need an app? What I'd do

If in doubt, start with the website. It brings in customers, you'll need it anyway and it shows you what your customers actually use. If over time you see them using you daily, or asking for something a website can't do, you have your reason for an app, with data behind it.
