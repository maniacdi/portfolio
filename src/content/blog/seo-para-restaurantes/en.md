Friday, quarter to nine, a couple of tourists by the beach search "restaurants near me" on their phone. Three listings appear on the map. They look at the photos, the rating, open one menu… and the menu is an 8 MB PDF that won't load on one bar of signal. On to the next. Restaurant SEO is decided in moments like that: thirty seconds, on a phone.

The good news is you don't need to be an expert. The bad news is that almost everything that works is ongoing upkeep, not a one-afternoon job. I'll go through it in the order I'd tackle it, from what moves the needle most to least. The general side is in my [local SEO guide](/en/blog/seo-local-negocios); this post is about what's specific to restaurants.

## Restaurant SEO starts with your Google Maps listing

For a restaurant on Google Maps, the listing is your shop window, your menu and your front door all at once. Before you touch the website:

- A primary category as specific as possible: "Seafood restaurant" or "Steakhouse" says far more than "Restaurant".
- Real kitchen hours, not the hours the doors are open. If the kitchen closes at 11pm and you say you're open until 1am, someone will turn up at 11:30 and tell the world in a review.
- Attributes: outdoor seating, vegetarian options, wheelchair access, dog-friendly. They're filters people actually use.
- A link to your menu and a booking link, both.
- Photos of dishes, the dining room and the front. Food photos uploaded by customers count too, and they're often what people look at most.

## Then, reviews

In hospitality, your rating and number of reviews matter more than in almost any other sector, because people compare five places in a minute. Ask when you bring the bill, with a QR code on the table or the receipt, and reply to every one. A polite reply to someone who complained about the wait is worth more than ten "thanks for coming".

And don't offer a free dessert in exchange for a review. Google bans it even if the review is honest.

## The menu as text, not a PDF

Look at restaurant websites in any town and you'll find this mistake over and over, and it's the easiest to fix. A menu as a PDF or a photo:

- Loads badly on mobile.
- Can't be read on a small screen without zooming.
- Is much harder for Google to understand than a normal page.

A menu written as a web page, with dishes, a line of description and prices, reads well on any phone and tells Google you do paella, Sunday roasts or gluten-free cooking. That's exactly what someone types when they search for it. If you change the menu every season, make sure the website lets you edit it yourself without waiting on anyone.

## A fast website that says where you are

To rank a restaurant on Google, the website doesn't need to be spectacular. It needs to load fast on mobile data, say on the homepage what you cook and where, and have a tappable phone number and a visible booking button. What else a restaurant website needs (gallery, events, set menu) I cover in [restaurant websites](/en/blog/web-para-restaurantes).

One technical detail I do recommend: Restaurant structured data, with cuisine, opening hours and a link to the menu. Customers never see it, and it saves Google from guessing. And keep your name, address and phone number written the same way on your site, your listing and any platform you appear on.

## Booking platforms: useful, but not yours

TheFork, Tripadvisor, OpenTable, local guides… Being on them with correct details helps, because they're mentions of your restaurant on sites Google knows. The problem is relying on them alone: the customer books on the platform, the platform keeps the relationship and, depending on the model, charges you per diner.

My take: use them as a shop window, and have your Google listing and website lead to your own booking system whenever you can. I go through the pros and cons in [reservations: platform or your own system](/en/blog/reservas-restaurante-plataforma-o-propio).

## What won't work

- Paying someone who promises to "put you first on Maps". Google doesn't sell those spots.
- One page per neighbourhood with the same text. It's filler, and Google knows it.
- Filling your listing description with "best restaurant in town". It reads terribly and doesn't help.

## If you only have one afternoon

Review your listing (category, kitchen hours, photos, booking link) and turn the PDF menu into text. Customers will notice both changes that same night, and Google within a few weeks.
