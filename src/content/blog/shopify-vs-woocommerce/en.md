Search for Shopify vs WooCommerce and you'll find dozens of comparisons, many of them carrying an affiliate link to one of the two. I don't earn a penny from either. I've built a store on Shopify ([Ū Skin Store](/en/projects/uskinstore), for a clinic in Santander) and I've written plenty about [why I don't like WordPress for a business website](/en/blog/web-a-medida-vs-wordpress), so you know which way I lean. Even so, WooCommerce has its place, and I'll tell you where.

## The fundamental difference

Shopify is a service: you pay a monthly fee and the store lives on their servers. They make sure it works, keep it secure and update it.

WooCommerce is a free WordPress plugin. You install it on your own hosting and everything is yours: the code, the data, and the problems too.

Almost every other difference follows from that.

## Shopify vs WooCommerce, side by side

| | Shopify | WooCommerce |
|---|---|---|
| Entry cost | Monthly plan fee | Free plugin, you pay for hosting |
| Extras | Apps, most with a monthly subscription | Extensions, many with an annual licence |
| Fees | The gateway's; extra if you don't use Shopify Payments | Only those of the gateway you choose |
| Maintenance | Shopify handles it | WordPress, theme and plugins are on you |
| Payment security | Included | Depends on your hosting and your updates |
| Control over the code | Limited to the theme and apps | Total |
| Ease of use | Simple admin, on mobile too | More options, steeper learning curve |

## What it really costs

On paper, WooCommerce wins: the plugin is free. In practice, a WooCommerce store needs decent hosting (the cheap kind struggles once the catalogue grows), paid extensions for shipping, invoices or filters, and someone to keep it updated. Shopify charges its fee, the apps and an extra fee per sale if you use a gateway other than its own.

My take: for a small store, once you add everything up, the cost of the two is closer than the entry price suggests. The difference is in what you pay with. With Shopify you pay in subscriptions; with WooCommerce, in someone's hours. What else goes into the sum (catalogue, payments, shipping) is in the [guide to starting an online store](/en/blog/como-montar-tienda-online).

## Maintenance and security, where it's really decided

This is where I come down on one side. A store handles orders, addresses and payments. Every WordPress plugin is one more door to keep updated, and WooCommerce stores tend to collect quite a few: payments, shipping, invoices, SEO, caching… If one falls behind on updates, the hole is in your store.

With Shopify that layer simply isn't your job. Apps don't install code on a server of yours and the checkout is handled by Shopify. You can break the theme, but you'd struggle to expose your customers' payments.

A WordPress update breaking the store on a Friday afternoon? It happens. Sound familiar?

## When WooCommerce makes sense

It isn't all Shopify. WooCommerce fits well when:

- You already have a WordPress site with lots of content and the store is a small part of it.
- You have someone you trust maintaining it, and you want full control of the code and the data.
- Your model needs something very specific that exists as a WooCommerce extension and would mean custom development on Shopify.

But the best ecommerce platform for a small business starting from scratch, with no existing website and nobody technical in-house, is still Shopify in my book. You set it up, you sell, and you get on with running your business.

## And if neither fits

Sometimes what you sell doesn't sit well in either: your own business rules, a management dashboard you already have, or a model that isn't a catalogue.
