As a developer, the easy answer would be to tell you everything should be custom-built. But when the Ū Medicina Estética clinic wanted to sell its products online, the store ended up on Shopify, with one part built on top. Shopify vs custom ecommerce isn't about which is better in the abstract. It's about how much of your business fits the mould, and what it costs you to maintain the part that doesn't.

I'll frame it with the questions I ask myself before recommending one or the other.

## Do you sell ordinary products, with a price and stock?

If what you sell is a catalogue of products with variants, prices, stock and shipping, Shopify has it covered from day one. And it covers it well:

- Hosting, SSL certificate and payment security, without you touching a server.
- A checkout tested by millions of shoppers, with cards, Apple Pay or Google Pay.
- An admin to manage orders, stock, customers and shipping, from your phone too.
- Constant updates that aren't your problem.

Those are the Shopify pros that matter most to a small shop, and almost none of them are visible: they're everything you don't have to maintain. A custom store would have to build each of those pieces and then look after them.

If that's you, my recommendation is Shopify. If you're torn between Shopify and WooCommerce, I compare those separately in [Shopify vs WooCommerce](/en/blog/shopify-vs-woocommerce).

## Does your business have a rule Shopify doesn't support?

This is where it gets interesting. At [Ū Skin Store](/en/projects/uskinstore), part of the catalogue can only be bought with a code the clinic hands out, because selective distribution brands don't allow those products to be sold to just anyone. Shopify doesn't do that out of the box.

So, without ditching Shopify, I built what was missing on top: a validation that runs inside Shopify itself and blocks payment if the customer doesn't have permission. I told the whole story in [hiding a product isn't protecting it](/en/blog/ocultar-el-producto-no-es-protegerlo), including the mistake that's tempting to make first.

That middle ground is what I recommend most often: Shopify for the common stuff, your own code only for what makes you different. What you need to know is where the ceiling is. Deep customisation of the checkout page, for example, is reserved for Shopify Plus, a plan aimed at large businesses and priced accordingly. If your rule has to live right in the checkout, find that out before you start.

## Is what you sell not really a product?

Shopify is designed for products. When you sell something else, the mould starts to pinch:

- Appointments, day rentals or limited places, where the calendar matters and stock doesn't.
- Configurable products with calculated prices (dimensions, materials, combinations that don't fit into variants).
- Stores that are one part of a bigger system: your own management dashboard, a loyalty scheme, or an ERP with its own rules.

You can bend Shopify with apps for almost anything, but every app is another monthly fee and another piece that depends on someone else. When the core of your business sits in what Shopify doesn't do, custom online store development starts to pay off.

## What does each option cost to run?

This is the question almost nobody asks at the start, and it's the one that matters most three years in.

With Shopify you pay the plan fee, the apps you install (most with their own subscription) and a fee per sale that depends on the payment gateway. In return, the technical maintenance is Shopify's job. What you, or whoever you hire, maintain is the theme and anything built on top.

With a custom store there's no platform fee and no Shopify commission, but you pay for hosting and, above all, for someone to maintain the code: security updates, changes to the payment provider's API, the library that stops being supported. If nobody does it, the store ages badly and quietly.

Neither option is free. The costs just sit in different places.

## Shopify vs custom ecommerce: what I'd decide

For a shop that's just starting: Shopify, with the theme adapted to your brand and nothing more. When a rule turns up that no sensible app covers, build it on top. And go custom only when what you sell isn't a catalogue, or the store is part of a system you already have.
