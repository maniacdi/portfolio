One of the most common mistakes in small online stores is pasting the manufacturer's description into every product page. It's understandable: you have two hundred products, the copy is already written and it's accurate. The problem is that every other store selling that product has the same text, and Google has no reason to show yours first. Ecommerce SEO starts right there: giving Google something only you have.

This is what I'd look at in a store, from the product page upwards.

## Product page SEO

The product page is the one that sells, and also the one that can catch the most specific searches ("vitamin C serum for sensitive skin", "waterproof women's trail shoes"). What matters:

- The product title, written the way someone would search for it: brand, product type and what sets it apart. Not an internal code.
- Your own description. You don't need to rewrite the whole catalogue on day one: start with your best sellers or the ones with the best margin. Add what you know and the manufacturer doesn't: who it's for, how to use it, what it goes with, what customers ask you at the counter.
- Lightweight photos with alt text that describes the product. A product page with ten 3 MB photos is a slow page, and a slow store loses sales before it loses rankings. I explain why in [a fast website ranks better](/en/blog/web-rapida-mejor-seo).
- Product structured data: price, availability and ratings in the format Google understands. It's what lets the price or the stars appear in the search result. The idea is the same as in [structured data for a local business](/en/blog/datos-estructurados-negocio-local), just with a different type.

## Category page SEO, the most underrated part

Category pages (collections, in Shopify) usually rank better than product pages for broad searches: "facial sunscreen", "stovetop espresso makers". And they're almost always empty: a title and a grid of products.

Two changes make the difference. First, organise categories the way people search, not the way your stockroom is arranged. Second, add a short text at the top or bottom: what's in that category, how to choose, who each option is for. Three useful paragraphs are enough.

## Duplicate content: the curse of being a store

Stores generate near-identical pages without anyone creating them on purpose. The usual suspects:

- Variants. A T-shirt in five colours doesn't need five pages: it's one product page with variants.
- The same product in several collections. Shopify handles this with a canonical tag that always points to the main product page; on a custom store, that's your job.
- Filters and sorting. Every filter combination can generate a new URL. If Google indexes them all, your store gets spread across hundreds of thin pages. It's best to keep combinations out of the index except the ones people actually search for.

## When you change or discontinue a product

In Shopify, a product page's URL comes from the product name. Change the name, the URL changes, and the old one disappears along with whatever it had ranked for. Shopify offers to create a redirect when you change it from the admin: always tick it. At [Ū Skin Store](/en/projects/uskinstore) this forced me to treat URLs as fixed in the import system, because an uncontrolled rename duplicates the product instead of updating it.

For discontinued products, it depends. If it'll be back in stock, leave the page up as sold out. If it won't, redirect to its replacement or its category. What I wouldn't do is just delete it and leave a 404 where there used to be visits.

## If you also have a physical shop

For a business with premises, the online store and local SEO help each other. Click and collect, your address and opening hours on the site, and a well-kept Google profile get you found by people searching nearby. All of that is in [local SEO for small businesses](/en/blog/seo-local-negocios).

## Where I'd start with your ecommerce SEO

Take your ten best-selling products and your three main categories. Write your own description for each product and a useful text for each category. Check that the photos are light and the store loads fast on mobile. It's not much work compared to the whole catalogue, and it's where it shows most.
