You open the website of a business in your town, spot "Blog" in the menu and click out of curiosity. Three posts: "Welcome to our new website!", "Merry Christmas" and one from four years ago about a course the owner went on. Sound familiar? That blog isn't bringing in customers. If anything, it tells visitors the website has been abandoned.

So before you start business blogging, ask yourself the awkward question: will I actually keep it going? I say that from experience, because I write this blog myself and I know what it takes to get a decent post out every few weeks on top of the day job.

## When business blogging does bring in customers

A blog works when your customers search Google for things your service pages don't answer. Questions, doubts, comparisons. Some businesses where it tends to fit:

- A clinic: "does a root canal hurt?", "how long does a sprained ankle take to heal". People search before they book.
- A garage or a building firm: "how often should I change my oil", "do I need planning permission for a new bathroom".
- A specialist shop: "which bike to buy as a beginner", "how to care for a linen sofa".
- Any service with unclear pricing: "how much does … cost" is one of the most common searches out there.

In all of these, the person searching is one step away from needing you. If you answer their question well, and locally, there's a good chance they end up on your listing or your contact form.

## When it's wasted time

If your business runs on impulse or pure proximity (a local pub, a bakery, a newsagent), a blog adds little. Nobody searches "how to choose a bakery". Your time is far better spent on your Google listing, photos and reviews, which I go through in my [local SEO guide](/en/blog/seo-local-negocios).

It's also not worth it if what you'll publish is business news ("new opening hours this month") or generic text lifted from other sites. The first belongs on your Google listing or social media. The second nobody will read, and Google has no reason to show it.

And if you know you won't write, don't start. No blog beats a dead blog.

## How to find topics people actually search for

The most reliable source isn't a tool: it's your counter. For a month, note down the questions customers ask you on the phone, by message or in person. Every repeated question is a post. If they're asking you, they're asking Google too.

Then a few quick checks to sharpen your blog ideas for a small business:

- Type the question into Google and look at the autocomplete suggestions and the "People also ask" box. They show you how people phrase it.
- Look at what currently ranks for it. If it's thin content or from another city, there's room for you.
- If your site is already connected to Search Console, look for searches you appear for without a page that answers them properly.

One detail that makes a difference for a local business: write from where you are. "What to do if your ground floor floods" is generic; explaining it with what happens in your area when it rains hard, and what you've seen yourself, is something nobody else can copy.

## How often to publish without burning out

My take, which some will disagree with: one good post a month beats four weak ones. Consistency matters, but it matters more that each post answers the question fully. A post that genuinely answers something people search for can keep bringing in visits for years.

A few things that help you keep going:

- Keep a list of topics ready. Sitting down to write without knowing what about is the hardest part.
- Write the way you talk to customers, not like a brochure. It's quicker and reads better.
- Make sure you can publish yourself without calling anyone. If every post depends on your web person, there won't be many. I cover that in [how to update your website without a developer](/en/blog/actualizar-web-sin-programador).
- Every post should lead somewhere: the related service page, the booking button, the phone number.

## How to tell if it's working

Give it time: a new post usually takes weeks or months to settle in Google. Then look at two things: whether posts get visits from search, and whether any of those visits turn into a call, a form or a booking. The first without the second is nice-looking traffic and not much else. How to measure all that without losing your mind is in [is your website bringing in customers?](/en/blog/medir-resultados-web-negocio).

## What I'd do

If your business answers questions every day, write down ten over a month and turn the most repeated one into a post. Publish it, link to it from the service page and see what happens before you commit to a schedule. If nobody asks you questions, forget the blog and spend that time on your Google listing.
